# Program structure

Forty courses run across four tracks and four levels. Math and statistics take 16 of the 30 core
courses, plus most track electives, because they are the biggest gap and the hardest to fake in an
admissions file.

| Track | L1 Foundations | L2 Core I | L3 Core II | L4 Specialization | Total |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Mathematics (M) | 3 | 2 | 2 | in track | 7+ |
| Statistics (S) | 1 | 2 | 4 | 2 | 9+ |
| Computer Science (C) | 3 | 3 | 2 | in track | 8+ |
| Business & Economics (B) | 3 | 3 | 2 | 1 | 9+ |
| Track electives + capstone | – | – | – | 7 | 7 |
| **Courses per level** | **10** | **10** | **10** | **10** | **40** |

## Every course follows the same 40 / 40 / 20 split

- **Theory (40%)** — textbook chapters, derivations, proof or problem sets graded against a solutions manual.
- **Practice (40%)** — a coded build in Python or R on a real dataset, pushed to GitHub with a README.
- **Business application (20%)** — a one-page memo or 5-slide deck answering "so what?" for a named decision-maker.

## Pacing

A course takes about 45 hours. The plan runs two courses in parallel at about 5 hours a week each:

- **Lane A — theory:** mathematics and statistics.
- **Lane B — applied:** computer science and business.

Each full course therefore spans 9 weeks; a *test-out* course spans 3. With a review/buffer week
every quarter and breaks for Holy Week, Christmas and New Year, the program takes about 198 weeks (just under 4 years). The exact calendar is generated in [`plan/`](../plan/).

**Fitting it around relocation.** Levels 1–2 are mostly math, which runs fine alongside a 2028
job-abroad push; Levels 3–4 can slow down after a move if hours get tight.

**Order rule:** never start a course before its prerequisites, and always pair a theory-heavy course
with a lighter applied one so momentum holds.
