# Priorities and proof for each master's path

Self-study builds the skill, but only credit-bearing courses and test scores get past an admissions
screen, so collect formal proof for the must-have courses on the chosen path.

| Path | Must-have courses | Can lighten | Proof to collect |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| MS Statistics + domain minor | M102, M201, M301, M202, S201, S301, S302, A1 Real Analysis | C201–C203, B301–B302 | For-credit university calculus, linear algebra and probability courses with a transcript; GRE quant near the top; optionally the GRE Mathematics Subject Test |
| MS Computer Science + statistics | M103, M202, C201, C202, C203, S201, S303, B1–B5 | B201–B203, B301–B302 | For-credit discrete math, data structures and algorithms courses; GRE quant; a strong GitHub portfolio |
| MBA, analytics concentration | B101–B302, B401, C1–C5, S302, S401, capstone | M301, M302, S301, A-track theory | GMAT or GRE; leadership evidence from consulting work; capstone client outcome |

**Fastest credible proof for the stats core.** The MITx MicroMasters in Statistics and Data Science
on edX covers probability, statistics theory and machine learning at MIT level, and certificates like
it carry more weight than self-study. Check its current availability and credit options before
enrolling.

**For Europe and Australia/NZ.** Universities there often assess applicants by the math, statistics
or CS credits on the bachelor's transcript, and a BSBA may fall short. Watch for three routes:
programs that accept relevant work experience, pre-master's or bridging programs (common in the
Netherlands), and for-credit single courses taken before applying. Check each target program's
requirements a year before applying.

**Decide the path by the end of Level 2.** Levels 1–2 are the same for all three; grades in M202 and
S201 show honestly whether the statistics path feels like a stretch or a fit.
